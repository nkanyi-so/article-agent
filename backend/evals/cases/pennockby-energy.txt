Morag Sewell: Welcome to the annual meeting of Pennockby Energy Co-operative. Devan will take us through the year's numbers.
Devan Ruhl: Thank you, Morag. I'll go slowly, because there are a lot of them.
Devan Ruhl: The turbine generated 3,120 megawatt hours in the year. The year before it was 2,870.
Greta Molnar: So up by 250.
Devan Ruhl: Yes, up by 250 megawatt hours. It was a windier year and we lost fewer days to repairs.
Morag Sewell: How many days were lost?
Devan Ruhl: The turbine was down for 11 days. The year before it was down for 26.
Devan Ruhl: Income from selling electricity was 412,000 pounds. Running costs were 148,000 pounds.
Greta Molnar: And the loan?
Devan Ruhl: We repaid 120,000 pounds of the loan. What we still owe is 540,000 pounds.
Morag Sewell: That leaves a surplus.
Devan Ruhl: It leaves a surplus of 144,000 pounds.
Greta Molnar: What happens to it?
Devan Ruhl: The board proposes three things. 60,000 pounds goes to members as interest, which is 4 percent on their shares. 50,000 pounds goes into the repair reserve. And 34,000 pounds goes to the community fund.
Morag Sewell: How many members do we have now?
Devan Ruhl: We have 1,840 members. Ninety-five joined during the year.
Greta Molnar: What did the community fund pay for last year?
Devan Ruhl: Last year the fund gave 28,000 pounds. The biggest grant was 9,500 pounds, for insulating the village hall.
Morag Sewell: And the reserve?
Devan Ruhl: The repair reserve stands at 210,000 pounds before this year's 50,000. A new gearbox would cost about 300,000 pounds, so we are not there yet.
Greta Molnar: When would we be?
Devan Ruhl: At 50,000 pounds a year, the reserve passes 300,000 in two years.
Morag Sewell: Thank you, Devan. We will vote on the three proposals after the break.
