Imogen Vasey: My guest is Aurelio Banks, director of the Calderwick Museum, which reopens next month. Aurelio, the museum has been shut for a long time. Tell me what happened.
Aurelio Banks: We closed in January last year after a water main burst under the east wing. The basement flooded to a depth of about a metre. We moved 9,000 objects out of the stores in four days, most of them by hand, with staff and volunteers working in shifts. Nothing was lost, which I still find hard to believe. About 200 objects needed conservation work, mostly paper and textiles that had got damp.
Imogen Vasey: And the building itself?
Aurelio Banks: The building was the bigger problem. The flood showed us that the wiring in the east wing dated from the 1960s and had to be replaced. So what began as a repair became a refit of the whole wing. The work cost 2.4 million pounds. The insurers paid 1.1 million of that, the council gave 800,000, and we raised the remaining 500,000 from donations. More than 3,000 people gave money, and the smallest gift was two pounds from a school class.
Imogen Vasey: What will visitors see that is new?
Aurelio Banks: The east wing now has a gallery about the town's mills, which we never had room for before. It holds a working loom that was built here in 1887 and has not run in public for fifty years. We have also moved the stores out of the basement to the second floor, so they are above any water. And for the first time there is a lift, so every floor can be reached without stairs.
Imogen Vasey: When do you open?
Aurelio Banks: We open on the fourteenth of next month. Entry stays free, as it has been since the museum was founded.
Imogen Vasey: Is there anything you would have done differently?
Aurelio Banks: I would have moved the stores years ago. We knew the basement was the wrong place for them. It took a flood to make us do it.
Imogen Vasey: Aurelio Banks, thank you.
Aurelio Banks: Thank you for having me.
