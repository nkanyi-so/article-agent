Ines Wetherby: Welcome back to the programme. My guest today is Rafael Dunmore, who founded Quillmoor Books. Rafael, thank you for coming in.
Rafael Dunmore: Thank you for having me.
Ines Wetherby: For listeners who haven't been, what is Quillmoor Books?
Rafael Dunmore: It's a bookshop on Tanner's Quay. We opened in 2019. We sell new books, and since last year we also run a subscription.
Ines Wetherby: Tell me about the subscription. How does it work?
Rafael Dunmore: You pay 18 pounds a month and we post you one book, chosen by one of our booksellers. You tell us three books you loved and one you hated, and we choose from there.
Ines Wetherby: One you hated?
Rafael Dunmore: The one you hated tells us more than the three you loved.
Ines Wetherby: That's a good line. When did you start it?
Rafael Dunmore: In March 2025. We started with 60 subscribers, all of them regular customers of the shop.
Ines Wetherby: And now?
Rafael Dunmore: We have 1,200 subscribers.
Ines Wetherby: That's a big jump in a short time. What drove it?
Rafael Dunmore: Mostly gifts. About 40 percent of our subscriptions are bought as a present for somebody else.
Ines Wetherby: Do gift subscribers stay?
Rafael Dunmore: Less than people who sign up for themselves. A gift usually runs for three or six months and then stops.
Ines Wetherby: So how many people leave in a typical month?
Rafael Dunmore: About 4 percent of subscribers cancel each month.
Ines Wetherby: Is that good or bad?
Rafael Dunmore: I honestly don't know. I don't have another bookshop's numbers to compare it with.
Ines Wetherby: Who chooses the books?
Rafael Dunmore: We have five booksellers, and each of them looks after a list of subscribers. Odette Marsh has been with us since the start and she has the longest list.
Ines Wetherby: How long does it take to choose a book for one person?
Rafael Dunmore: Odette says about ten minutes for a new subscriber and two or three minutes once she knows them.
Ines Wetherby: That is a lot of minutes across 1,200 people.
Rafael Dunmore: It is. Choosing is now most of what the booksellers do on a Monday and a Tuesday.
Ines Wetherby: Has it changed the shop itself?
Rafael Dunmore: Yes. The subscription is now a little over half of our income. The shop floor is the smaller part.
Ines Wetherby: Does that worry you?
Rafael Dunmore: A bit. If the post gets slower or dearer, that hurts us more than it used to.
Ines Wetherby: What does postage cost you?
Rafael Dunmore: About 3 pounds 20 a parcel. It was 2 pounds 85 when we started.
Ines Wetherby: Have you raised the price to cover it?
Rafael Dunmore: Not yet. We have held it at 18 pounds since the start.
Ines Wetherby: Will you?
Rafael Dunmore: Probably next year. I haven't decided by how much.
Ines Wetherby: What's the most common complaint?
Rafael Dunmore: That we sent somebody a book they already own. It happens about once in every 50 parcels.
Ines Wetherby: What do you do when it happens?
Rafael Dunmore: We send another book free and ask them to pass the first one on to a friend.
Ines Wetherby: Has anyone tried to copy you?
Rafael Dunmore: I'm sure they have. I'd say good luck to them. The hard part isn't the idea, it's having booksellers who read.
Ines Wetherby: What's next for Quillmoor?
Rafael Dunmore: We are opening a second shop, in Lowther Arcade, in the spring. And we are starting a children's subscription in September.
Ines Wetherby: Same price?
Rafael Dunmore: No. The children's one will be 12 pounds a month.
Ines Wetherby: Rafael Dunmore, thank you.
Rafael Dunmore: Thank you, Ines.
